>The United States had made slave traffic illegal in 1808, and occasionally contributed a sloop or two to the slave patrols: but slavery itself was still legal in the southern States, the American Ambassador in London was a Virginian, and the Americans had never conceded the Royal Navy's right of search, so that every interception was a diplomatic gamble. American slavers had the best ships, too-especially Baltimore clippers and New York sloops, which were among the fastest vessels afloat, and could easily out-manoeuvre the clumsy broad-beamed brigs of the patrols. One successful American slaver was the schooner *Wanderer*, built as a pleasure-yacht and owned by a Georgia slaving syndicate: she flew the pennant of the New York Yacht Club, and her master once entertained the officers of a Royal Navy frigate to a merry dinner on board, before packing 750 slaves below deck and sailing for home.[^1]
